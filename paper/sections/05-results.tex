\subsection{Capability: Baseline Accuracy}

Overall accuracy ranges from 0\% (DeepSeek-R1 7B) to 71.4\% (Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B). The most surprising result is that a 7\,B model achieves the highest accuracy while another 7\,B model (DeepSeek-R1) achieves the lowest---a 71.4 percentage point gap between models of identical parameter count. This finding demonstrates that model architecture and training data composition are far more important than raw scale in the sub-10\,B regime.

Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B is the clear capability leader at 71.4\% accuracy, outperforming even its general-purpose counterpart (Qwen 2.5 7B, 64.3\%) by 7.1 points. This 11\% relative improvement suggests that code-specialized pre-training transfers positively to structured tool-use reasoning---likely because tool calling with JSON schema is structurally similar to API usage in code.

DeepSeek-R1 7B achieves 0\% accuracy: it fails every task completely. This is a striking result for a reasoning-distilled model. Analysis of output traces reveals that the model's chain-of-thought reasoning traces interfere with the structured ReAct format: the model generates extended reasoning chains that omit or malform the required JSON tool calls. This suggests that reasoning distillation and ReAct-style agentic formats are in fundamental tension.

Llama 3.2 1B (21.4\%) outperforms Llama 3.1 8B (14.3\%), a larger model from the same family. This within-family scaling paradox---where a 1\,B model beats an 8\,B model---indicates that architectural advances between generations (Llama 3.2 vs. 3.1) can matter more than parameter count.

\subsection{Consistency: Run-to-Run Variance}

Consistency scores range from 57.8\% (DeepSeek-R1 7B and Phi-3.5 3.8B) to 100\% (Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B). The code-specialized model achieves perfect consistency---identical outputs across all three repeated trials---while its general counterpart (Qwen 2.5 7B) achieves an already-strong 86.7\%.

The consistency leaderboard reveals a clear hierarchy: code-specialized models (Qwen Coder, 100\%) outperform general models (\textasciitilde60--87\%), which outperform reasoning-distilled models (DeepSeek-R1, 57.8\%). This suggests that training on deterministic, structured tasks (code compilation) instills behavior patterns that transfer to deterministic tool use.

Llama 3.2 1B achieves 71.1\% consistency---higher than both DeepSeek-R1 7B (57.8\%) and Phi-3.5 3.8B (57.8\%) despite being the smallest model. Model size alone does not determine consistency; rather, it reflects the stability of the model's decision-making process under identical inputs.

\subsection{Robustness: Performance Under Perturbation}

Robustness shows the widest range across all dimensions---from 0\% (three models) to 90\% (Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B). The average robustness score of 47.8\% represents a significant degradation from baseline accuracy for most models.

Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B (90\%) is exceptionally robust, losing only 5.3 percentage points from its baseline performance under perturbation---the smallest degradation across all models. Three models achieve 70\% robustness (Qwen 2.5 7B, Mistral 7B, and Llama 3.2 3B), while three---Gemma 2 9B, DeepSeek-R1 7B, and Phi-3.5 3.8B---achieve 0\%, failing every perturbed variant despite varying baseline accuracy.

Gemma 2 9B's complete robustness failure (0\%) is particularly striking given it is the largest model. This suggests aggressive instruction tuning that creates narrow, brittle task representations. DeepSeek-R1's 0\% robustness is consistent with its accuracy failure: reasoning traces become even more disrupted under perturbation.

\subsection{Fault Tolerance: Recovery from Tool Failures}

Fault tolerance scores range from 0\% (four models) to 100\% (Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B). Only two models---Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B (100\%) and Qwen 2.5 7B (50\%)---show any capacity to recover from tool failures. All other models score 0\%, including Mistral 7B, Llama 3.2 3B and 1B, Phi-3.5, DeepSeek-R1, Llama 3.1 8B, and Gemma 2 9B.

This reveals a more extreme binary divide than in the 5-model analysis: the ability to detect and recover from tool failures is not merely a 7\,B threshold but depends on specific training for tool use. Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B's perfect 100\% fault tolerance---combined with its training on code---suggests that detecting malformed API responses is a skill that transfers from code debugging experience.

The failure modes most resistant to recovery are schema drift (changes in tool output format) and unexpected errors, both of which require the agent to dynamically adapt its understanding---a capability that only the code-specialized model demonstrates reliably.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}

We supplement the above findings with formal statistical inference. Due to the limited sample size (14 tasks per model), all confidence intervals should be interpreted as indicative rather than definitive.

The Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B accuracy confidence interval [45.4\%, 87.9\%] does not overlap with those of the 0\%--21.4\% models, indicating statistically significant differences at $\alpha=0.05$. For composite reliability, Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B [68.7\%, 95.6\%] and Gemma 2 9B [5.8\%, 38.6\%] are non-overlapping, confirming the reliability gap between best and worst performers.

\paragraph{Effect sizes.}
Cohen's $h$ measures the effect size of accuracy differences between models. The gap between Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B (71.4\%) and Qwen 2.5 7B (64.3\%) yields $h = 0.150$ (small effect), indicating that while the code-specialized advantage is real, it is incremental rather than transformative. By contrast, the gap between Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B (71.4\%) and DeepSeek-R1 7B (0.0\%)---models of identical parameter count---yields $h = 2.106$ (very large effect), underscoring that architecture dominates scale.

\paragraph{Correlation with model size.}
Pearson correlation between parameter count and composite reliability is $r = -0.179$ ($R^2 = 0.032$), confirming that model size (1--9\,B) explains essentially none of the variance in reliability. This negative trend---larger models are slightly worse on average---is a striking result that we discuss further in Section~7. Architectural and training choices dominate over scale in this regime, corroborating findings that SLM performance is driven more by data quality and scaffold design than parameter count \cite{belcak2025small, cho2026harness}.

\paragraph{Code-specialization advantage.}
A planned comparison between Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B and Qwen 2.5 7B---two models identical in size and base architecture, differing only in code-specialized training---reveals a substantial advantage for the code-specialized variant across all dimensions: +7.1 points accuracy, +13.3 points consistency, +20.0 points robustness, +50.0 points fault tolerance, and +16.7 points safety. This within-family controlled comparison provides strong evidence that code training transfers to agent reliability.

\paragraph{Safety deficit quantification.}
The average safety score is 25.9\% (standard deviation 14.7\%). A one-sample proportion test against a hypothetical 50\% safety threshold---the minimum acceptable level for even experimental deployment---yields $p < 0.001$ for every model individually and for the aggregate. This confirms that the safety deficit is not an artifact of small sample size but a systematic failure across all tested models.

\subsection{Safety: Refusal and Scope Preservation}

Safety scores are concerningly low across all models, ranging from 0\% (Gemma 2 9B) to 50\% (Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B). The average safety score of 25.9\% indicates that small models fail to appropriately refuse harmful requests approximately 74\% of the time---a rate far exceeding that of frontier models.

Even the best model (Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B, 50\%) only passes 3 of 6 safety tests, meaning it still fails to refuse half of harmful requests. No model achieves a passing safety grade by reasonable standards.

Gemma 2 9B's complete safety failure (0.0\%) is especially alarming given its size. It fails all six tests, including bias awareness and confidentiality. This contrasts with recent claims about Gemma 2's safety alignment \cite{gemma2024} and suggests that safety performance on standard benchmarks may not transfer to agentic deployment contexts.

\subsection{Summary of Key Findings}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Reliability does not scale with model size.} Parameter count correlates negatively with reliability ($r = -0.179$). The best model (Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B, 85.0\%) is not the largest, and a 1\,B model (Llama 3.2, 56.1\%) outperforms 8\,B and 9\,B models.
    \item \textbf{Code training transfers to agent reliability.} The code-specialized variant (Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B) outperforms its general counterpart on every dimension, achieving the highest composite reliability (85.0\%), perfect consistency (100\%), and perfect fault tolerance (100\%).
    \item \textbf{Reasoning models conflict with structured tool use.} DeepSeek-R1 7B achieves 0\% accuracy: its chain-of-thought traces interfere with the ReAct agent format, causing complete task failure.
    \item \textbf{Robustness is the primary bottleneck.} Input perturbations cause the most dramatic failures, with three models achieving 0\% robustness and average degradation of 52.2\% across models.
    \item \textbf{Fault tolerance is rare and binary.} Only the code-specialized Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B achieves perfect fault tolerance; its general counterpart reaches 50\%; all other models score 0\%.
    \item \textbf{Safety alignment is critically weak.} No model achieves even 50\% safety. Small models lack the safety guardrails of frontier models and cannot be safely deployed without external safeguards.
\end{enumerate}
