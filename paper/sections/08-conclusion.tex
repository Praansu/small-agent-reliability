\section{Conclusion}

We presented the first comprehensive, multi-dimensional reliability evaluation of small language models as autonomous agents. Across nine models (1\,B--9\,B parameters), 31 tool-use tasks, and four reliability dimensions (consistency, robustness, fault tolerance, and safety), we found that:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item Reliability does not simply scale with parameter count---correlation is weakly negative ($r = -0.179$), capability--size correlation is non-significant ($r = 0.289$, $p = 0.451$), a 1\,B model achieves higher composite reliability than 8\,B and 9\,B models, and identical-size 7\,B models span 37.8\% to 85.0\% composite reliability and 25.8\% to 67.7\% capability.
    \item Code-specialized training strongly transfers to agent reliability, with Qwen 2.5 Coder 7B achieving the highest composite (85.0\%), perfect consistency (100\%), and perfect fault tolerance (100\%).
    \item Reasoning-distilled models (DeepSeek-R1 7B) fundamentally conflict with ReAct-style agentic formats, achieving the lowest capability (25.8\%) and 0\% accuracy on the reliability suite.
    \item Robustness under input perturbations shows the widest variance of any dimension (0--90\%, mean 47.8\%), with the largest model collapsing to 0\%.
    \item Safety failures affect all models at rates (50--100\% failure to refuse) that are unacceptable for production deployment; the pooled safety rate of 14/54 (25.9\%) is significantly below a 50\% threshold ($p = 0.0003$, one-sided binomial test).
    \item Statistically, the 31-task suite separates the extremes with confidence (only the two weakest models differ from the leader after multiple-testing correction), but mid-tier ordering remains unresolved at this sample size---underscoring the need for larger evaluation suites.
\end{enumerate}

As small language models continue their rapid adoption in edge devices, privacy-sensitive applications, and cost-constrained deployments, understanding and improving their reliability is essential. We hope our benchmark, findings, and mitigation strategies provide a foundation for building small-model agents that are not just capable, but reliably deployable in the real world.
